        Etymology & Realmslore

        Sources and Canon

        Realmslore is everything published about the Forgotten Realms, official or licensed. The Forgotten Realms Wiki ranks its sources, most trusted first: TSR and Wizards of the Coast books, novels and magazines about the Realms; Ed Greenwood's own writing and answers; general D&D books; books from other D&D settings; licensed games and comics; and online posts by other Realms authors.

        Ed Greenwood created the Realms, and what he writes or says about them counts as canon until an official book says otherwise. His answers to fans on the Candlekeep forums and the books he publishes himself are not official publications, though, so names that come only from them are labelled.

        Some naming rules come from D&D books that were not written for any one setting, such as the Player's Handbook and Races of the Wild.

        Elves (Tel’Quessir)

        The elves of Faerûn include the surface elves and the drow, who share a common ancestry. The notes here apply to both; the naming of each is described in its own section below, including related lore.

        Elves are given childhood names at birth and choose their adult names some time after their hundredth birthday (Player's Handbook, 5e). This generator creates only adult names, but childhood names are generally shorter. Although an elf's childhood name does not have to carry over into adulthood, the prefixes generated here could often serve as childhood names.

        Every elf has a family name, often translated into Common (Player's Handbook, 5e).

        With Expanded Variety on, some prefixes and suffixes are instead the start or end of a documented name; their meanings are unknown.

        No two elves share a name except by choice (The Complete Book of Elves, 2e). If a generated name matches that of a published character, it is flagged so you know.

        Surface Elves

        Surface elves have several lineages, the most common being high elves (sun and moon elves) and wood elves. There are no distinct naming conventions explicitly documented for these lineages (Elves of Evermeet and The Complete Book of Elves, 2e; Races of Faerûn, 3e). The Complete Book of Elves describes one fashion for all elves, soft sounds with the occasional hard consonant. The generator therefore uses a shared pool of names with a slight weight toward the published names of the lineage you choose.

        Wood elves have fewer published names than sun or moon elves, so their results draw more on the shared pool.

        First names are built from the elven name tables in Races of the Wild (3.5e), first published as "By Any Other Name" (Dragon #251): a prefix and one or more suffixes, each with its own meaning. When two components don't join smoothly, a letter is added, as that article suggests. Together with the family names, that allows over a quadrillion theoretically possible combinations.

        This generator uses published family names (with their translation when a book gives one, such as Galanodel, "Moonwhisper"), builds new ones from the same tables, or combines Common words found in published family names. In published sources, moon elves use Common family names more frequently than sun elves, and the generator reflects that distinction.

        Drow

        Drow are dark elves whose ancestral homeland is the Underdark. While they share a common ancestry with surface elves, they have developed their own distinct culture and traditions.

        Drow names are constructed using the tables in "By Any Other Name: The Drow" (Dragon #267), combining a prefix with one or more suffixes, each carrying its own meaning. Traditional drow naming conventions distinguish between masculine and feminine name elements, reflecting the rigid gender hierarchy of Lolthite society. Feminine elements carry associations of status and authority: a drow man who used one would be considered "a rogue or troublemaker."

        While noble families proudly bear their house surnames, commoners generally cannot use a noble house name unless they hold a titled position within that house (The Drow of the Underdark, 2e). This generator uses published house names or constructs new ones from the tables in Dragon #267. By tradition, no house name begins with the same "L" sound as Lolth, and no drow bears her name or its variants, except a revealed avatar of the goddess (The Drow of the Underdark, 2e).

        Espruar

        Espruar is the elven alphabet of Faerûn, the script elves use to write their languages (Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting, 3e). It is an alphabet, not a language.

        Under each name, the same letters are shown in an Espruar font. This is a letter-for-letter transcription, not a translation. Drow names are shown in the High Drowic font instead.

        The Lore Gem (Selu’kiira)

        The "Roll Name" button is inspired by the selu’kiira, an elven High Lore Gem. Made from rainbow tourmaline, these magical crystals preserve centuries of knowledge and memories, changing colors as they absorb more knowledge until they finally turn brilliant red after accumulating more than 3,000 years' worth (Cormanthyr: Empire of the Elves (1998), p. 153).

        Dwarves (Stout Folk)

        Races of Faerûn (3e) identifies shield dwarves (mountain or northern), gold dwarves (hill or southern), wild dwarves (jungle or Dur Authalar), duergar (dark or gray), urdunnir (deep or orecutter), and arctic dwarves (Inugaakalikurit). The latter two are rare, so this generator focuses on the first four. While they share a common ancestry, their naming traditions reflect cultural and societal differences.

        Unlike elves, who traditionally bear unique names, dwarves pass their names down through generations. A dwarf's first name is traditionally bestowed by a clan elder and belongs to the clan rather than the individual (Player's Handbook, 5e). As such, this generator uses published first names by default, each shown with the book it comes from. Only three published dwarf names are given for both men and women, and the Races of Stone tables mark every name male or female by its suffix, so dwarf names here are female or male, with no unisex option.

        Among dwarves it is more common to use one's clan name, as in Dorn Bladebite. A dwarf who is clanless or outlawed, or who wants to hide their clan, may use a kingdom name instead, as in Dorn of the Deep Realm (Dwarves Deep, 2e). Wild dwarves have no clans.

        To tell apart dwarves who share a name, shield and gold dwarves add phrases: a personal qualifier such as "the Younger" or "Hammerhand"; their ancestry, as "son of" or "daul of" (daughter of) a notable ancestor, or "blood of" for the most famous heroes and rulers; and the kingdom or hold they come from. These can be strung together (Dwarves Deep, 2e). About one name in ten gets such a phrase here, and the + button beside a name adds one. Places come from published Realms sources, such as "of Gauntlgrym".

        With Expanded Variety on, first names may also be constructed using the dwarven name tables in Races of Stone (3.5e), first published in Dragon #261: a prefix and a suffix, each with its own meaning. The suffixes differ between masculine and feminine names. Some prefixes and suffixes are instead the start or end of a documented name; their meanings are unknown. If a constructed name matches that of a published character, it is flagged so you know.

        Shield Dwarves

        Shield dwarves are the mountain dwarves of the north. Dwarves Deep (2e) prints a northern list of names and clans for them, and no name in it also appears in the southern list used for gold dwarves. This generator uses published names and clans, favoring those the sources tie to shield dwarves, but names and clans with no sourced ties to either lineage can appear for both.

        Shield dwarf names take the phrases described above, up to one per name. That limit is this generator's choice, not a rule the sources state.

        Gold Dwarves

        Gold dwarves are the hill dwarves of the south. Dwarves Deep (2e) prints a southern list of names and clans for them, separate from the northern list used for shield dwarves. This generator favors the names and clans the sources tie to gold dwarves; similarly, names and clans with no sourced ties to either lineage can appear for both.

        Gold dwarves place particular importance on ancestry and social standing, maintaining genealogies that span thousands of years. As a result, their names tend to be longer and more elaborate than those of other dwarves (Dwarves Deep, 2e).

        Gold dwarves live in the Great Rift, the Deep Realm and the Deep Lands beneath the Shaar, so "of the Deep Realm" suits them well. To reflect their longer names, this generator lets a gold dwarf name carry up to two of the phrases described above. That limit is this generator's choice, not a rule the sources state: Dwarves Deep's own example of a full gold name carries four.

        Wild Dwarves

        Wild dwarves inhabit the jungles of Faerûn. Unlike other dwarves, they do not organize themselves into clans, instead considering themselves one extended family, dur Authalar, meaning "the People" (Dwarves Deep, 2e).

        Only 52 wild dwarf names are documented (26 male and 26 female), so this generator draws on those alone: with Expanded Variety on, new names are made only from pieces of them, never from the dwarven name tables. In keeping with their traditions, no clan name is assigned.

        Duergar

        Duergar, also known as gray dwarves, descend from the shield dwarves of Clan Duergar, who were enslaved by mind flayers for thousands of years. Following their liberation, they established their own kingdoms in the Underdark, developing distinct cultural, linguistic and religious traditions (Races of Faerûn, 3e).

        Unlike surface dwarves, duergar make no distinction between masculine and feminine name elements in their published naming tables (Dragon #281). With Expanded Variety on, first names may be constructed using prefixes and suffixes from these tables, each with its own meaning.

        Duergar may identify themselves by paternal ancestry using "Than," meaning "child of," as in Sturgor Than Udubyr (Dragon #281). They also favor surnames that reflect their professions and craftsmanship (Underdark, 3e); this generator uses published duergar clan names only.

        Dethek

        Dethek is the universal dwarven alphabet of Faerûn, a runic script also used to write several other languages (Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting, 3e). It is an alphabet, not a language. All Dwarvish in Faerûn is written in the Dethek runic script, even though Dwarvish has distinct regional dialects.

        Under each dwarven name, the same letters are shown in a Dethek font. This is a letter-for-letter transcription, not a translation.
